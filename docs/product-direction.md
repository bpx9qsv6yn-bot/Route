# FairRoute product direction

## The core job

Help a person find a service they can realistically reach, understand the effort
and access uncertainty, and take a concrete next step. The resident route finder
is the product. Citywide analysis and intervention modeling are secondary tools.

## Main experience

- Open directly to the map and search panel, with a labeled public starting place.
- Search a need or a named service; change the starting place and travel limits.
- Update results immediately, without redoing route calculations for display-only changes.
- Keep map stations and the result list connected. Selecting either opens the same trip.
- Show trip steps, effort, access uncertainties, service information, and contact actions.
- Offer an explicit trip-summary download. Keep exact custom coordinates out of it.
- Explain why no route fits without silently relaxing an access requirement.

Mini Metro informs the graphic language: spare paper surfaces, clear colored
lines, geometric stations, and a small number of controls. Geographic routing
remains geographic; decorative schematics must not stand in for directions.

## What determines real-world quality next

1. **Current transit data.** The official fall 2026 feed is bundled, with selected service calendars checked.
   Maintain that refresh process and validate date-specific service before treating
   estimates as travel guidance. Live departures require a separate integration.
2. **Service and access verification.** Directory tags are not proof of eligibility,
   hours, capacity, or a usable entrance. Build a provider-verified update workflow
   and expose freshness for each field. Do not infer accessible facilities from
   missing data, and do not equate a general basic-needs tag with food service.
3. **Resident validation.** Test finding a service, changing mobility requirements,
   and understanding uncertainty with residents who use transit and mobility devices.
   Measure task completion and mistaken interpretations, then revise the flow.
4. **Route validation.** Check modeled walking legs against actual entrances,
   sidewalks, curb cuts, and transfer conditions. Street geometry alone cannot
   establish physical accessibility.

Additional dashboards, AI explanations, and allocation features should not displace
these requirements. A simpler product with trustworthy trips is the target.
